This is the framework, not the finished plan. It takes the three gaps the Combined Journey Map found and turns them into a reusable structure for deciding who gets engaged, when, how, and by whom - built so a future role (an investor, for instance) can be added later by extending the structure, not rewriting it. The actual message wording, any new tracked field, and any new SOP clause are deliberately left for the next round, once this shape has been reviewed.

The pattern, not just the plan

The Startup, the Referring Partner, and the Receiving Partner can share one timeline in the Combined Journey Map because they genuinely live through the same lead, stage for stage. That won't be true of every role this program adds later. An investor, for example, wouldn't experience "Origination" or "Acknowledge" at all - they'd have their own entirely different stages (something closer to sourcing, diligence, commitment, and portfolio support). So the reusable part of this framework isn't the six Lead stages. It's the idea that every role gets its own timeline of stages, and every stage of every role's timeline gets the same five questions asked of it.

Where this closes the three findings

Finding 1 (a lead that quietly times out, or gets declined, tells the referring partner nothing) is addressed by one proposed row: a message to the referring partner on any of the three Acknowledge-stage outcomes - accepted, declined, or timed out - not three separate messages, one trigger with three possible outcomes.

Finding 2 (no message for the moment credit actually lands) is addressed by one proposed row: a message to the referring partner the moment admin confirms conversion, distinct from both the existing pre-acknowledgment reminder and the much-later annual payout notice.

Finding 3 (a startup's repeat engagement isn't tracked) is the one gap this framework can name but not close by itself. A message can't be designed for an event nothing currently detects - this needs a tracked signal on the startup side first (something like the partner-side last_lead_shared_date and giving_pattern, but for a startup returning or leaving), which is a Data Dictionary change, not a messaging one. Flagged here as the first thing the next round needs to define, before any Stage 6 startup message can be written.

What this round deliberately leaves out

This document defines the shape - which role, which stage, which objective, which gap. It doesn't yet contain the actual message wording for any Proposed row, and it doesn't yet add the new Data Dictionary field Finding 3 needs. Those are real, concrete next steps, not open-ended - specifically: draft wording for the 4 clearly-scoped proposed messages (Stage 1 and 2 referring-partner confirmations, the Stage 3 outcome message, and the Stage 4 conversion message), define the startup-side tracking field(s) Finding 3 needs, and decide whether a startup gets a channel at all before writing anything to send them. Once drafted, these go directly into the Communication Template Library, the Data Dictionary, and Partnership SOP v1.0 directly, marked draft until confirmed - the same way every other drafted rule in this hub already works, not as a separate proposal layer.
