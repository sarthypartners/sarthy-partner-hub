This is the complete rulebook for how the partner program runs - the operational heart of Strategic Partners Playbook 3.0. It fully absorbs Sarthy's original Playbook 2.0, which is now retired. Each section's colored tag shows its status: green means confirmed, amber means drafted and awaiting Sarthy's formal sign-off. Anything still genuinely unresolved sits in one short note at the end of its section, rather than mixed into the rule itself - the Gap & Risk Register and Phase 0 Discussion Doc hold the full list if you want it in one place.

Quick summary, if you only read one thing: Sarthy connects startups with vetted service partners across Marketing, Finance, Legal, Technology, and Strategy. A partner joins by paying a small fee (₹999 India, $49 international) and gets 999 starting credits. From there, two separate rewards exist side by side: (1) share a lead with another partner, and once it converts, earn 999 credits minus Sarthy's 9% fee; (2) if that lead becomes a real signed deal, also earn 10% of the deal value minus Sarthy's 9% cut of that, paid out once a year. Credits never expire if paid for, always cost 999 to receive a lead, and a balance can only run negative so far before the account freezes. Leads must be genuine, disputes go through the admin within a day, and going quiet for 3 months puts an account to sleep until the partner comes back. A handful of details - which exchange rate applies at payout, what happens in a few edge-case disputes - are still waiting on Sarthy's final word; each is flagged individually below.

1. What this document is for confirmed

One place that explains how partners join, how money and credits work, how we keep them engaged, how we group them, and what to do when something goes wrong. Building the marketplace app itself is not covered here - that's the developers' job, not ours.

2. Where this program came from, and why it changed confirmed

Sarthy spent over three years working closely with early-stage and growth-stage startups, and kept seeing the same problem: a startup's biggest obstacle usually isn't ambition, it's not knowing the right professional help at the right time. The partner program exists to close that gap.

The first version of the program (2023-24) only flowed one way: Sarthy referred startups to partners, and partners never referred business to each other. The redesign turns the network from a one-way list of vendors into a system where any partner can refer to, and receive from, any other partner, with Sarthy tracking it all.

What changedFirst versionCurrent design
StructureSarthy to partner onlyAny partner to any partner, with Sarthy tracking everything
Cost to joinFree₹999 (India) or $49 (international)
Credit walletNoneStarts with 999 credits, earned and spent through referrals
How partners earnOnly from their own client workCredits for every referral that converts, plus their own client work

In one line: in the old model, a partner was a vendor. In the current design, a partner is a node in a shared network where everyone has something to gain and something to manage.

3. Who does what confirmed

Lead handles the overall plan, writing things down, reporting to Sarthy, writing the technical requirements, and deciding how to handle problems. Associate makes calls, enters data, and does the first round of follow-up with partners. Sarthy decides the money rules, the app's roadmap, and how success is measured, and stays involved as the programme's referee - every lead exchange runs through Sarthy's tracking, not directly between partners.

4. What partners actually get out of joining confirmed

  • Access to Sarthy's own base of early-stage and growth-stage startup clients
  • The right to send qualified leads to any other partner and earn credits when those leads convert
  • Incoming leads from other partners - a second source of new business beyond their own outreach
  • Transparent tracking - leads, conversions, and credits are all managed by Sarthy, not left to informal trust
  • A chance to be featured as Partner of the Month, a recognition program showcasing partner work to Sarthy's whole startup network
  • A listing in Sarthy's partner directory, with their case study and profile visible to startups

A partner who consistently sends leads that convert builds up real value; a partner who only ever receives without referring back will draw their balance down over time. The system rewards two-way participation, not one-sided use.

Paying the fee activates a partner's account and their starting 999 credits immediately. It doesn't guarantee any leads - it buys entry and a starting balance, which the partner grows through actually participating.

Both programmes grant 999 credits regardless of the fee difference, and the credit count never reprices - 999 stays 999 no matter what the INR/USD exchange rate does in the meantime. A partner's programme choice is locked permanently at sign-up. Currency only enters the picture once, at the moment of withdrawal (Section 10) - the credit itself stays a neutral unit the whole time in between, including when it moves between an India-programme partner and an international one.

Joining steps: apply through LinkedIn, a mailer, the WhatsApp community, or a direct approach → discovery call → Sarthy evaluates fit (service relevance, affordability, client diversity, professional standing, Giver/Taker read - Section 11) → agreement signed and uploaded → case study and profile submitted → fee paid → wallet activates with 999 credits → officially listed.

Every partner has a credit wallet Sarthy maintains, recording every credit added or taken away.

The credit amount is always 999, regardless of deal size or service category - simple and fair by design. Credits a partner paid real money for don't expire and roll over indefinitely. Credits Sarthy hands out as a goodwill gesture expire 30 days from issue. There's no cap on how large a balance can grow.

7. How a lead moves from one partner to another drafted, one mechanism still open

All referrals go through Sarthy - two partners privately swapping leads outside the system doesn't count and won't be honoured at settlement. A valid lead is a real prospect with an actual need, a real point of contact, and one that hasn't already been referred through the system or already engaged with that partner.

  • Cancelling a submission: a referring partner can cancel a lead before it reaches the receiving partner.
  • Duplicate leads: caught and flagged as invalid at the point of upload, before they go anywhere. Partners can also see a list of existing leads to reduce accidental duplicates.
  • Repeated declines: four or more leads declined in a row gets flagged to the admin, who steps in manually - not an automatic penalty, a prompt for a human to check.
  • Re-referring an unconverted lead: no fixed limit, but never to two partners offering the same type of service at once, since that creates competing outreach to the same prospect. Different types of service providers at once is fine.
  • Who picks the receiving partner: stays fully in the referring partner's hands, not Sarthy's - see Section 15 for how Sarthy's team supports this.

Still being worked out, not decided yet: what happens to a disputed lead past the 1-week window - the leading idea is admin discretion backed by a direct check-in with the startup to confirm what actually happened, but exactly how that evidence should work isn't finalized (full detail in the BRD, Section 11). Separately, a backend "catalyst" that surfaces good matches to partners is a real direction, not yet designed - to be explicit about the boundary: the system may suggest a match, but never assigns or auto-selects a receiving partner on someone's behalf. The referring partner always makes the final choice, even when a suggestion is shown.

8. The six credit rules drafted, all six specified

  1. Opening balance: every partner starts with 999 credits on payment. Not refundable.
  2. One credit per lead: conversion is confirmed by Sarthy based on proof from both partners - a signed agreement, an invoice, or similar.
  3. No double credits: one lead generates one deduction and one credit, ever.
  4. Credits can't be transferred between partners - only earned through real referrals, only cashed out at settlement.
  5. Fixed value: 999 credits per transaction, always, regardless of category or deal size.
  6. Negative balances: a balance can run down to -2,997 credits. Beyond that, the account freezes until topped up back to +999 - a top-up costs 4 times the entry fee (₹3,996 or $196) and adds 3,996 credits, landing exactly at +999.

9. How conversion gets confirmed, and what happens if someone disagrees confirmed for now

Sarthy has the final say on whether a lead converted. The same conversion standard applies across every service category for now - first payment plus a digitally signed agreement completed through the platform, which means the platform needs real digital-signature capability, not just an uploaded PDF.

If a partner disagrees, they can raise it in writing within 14 days. The admin responds within 24 hours, engaging both parties directly - this can happen offline, over email, no formal in-platform process required. The admin's decision is final for routine disputes. For serious or high-value disputes, a partner can escalate beyond the admin to Sarthy leadership directly - still open: the exact threshold for what counts as "serious or high-value" hasn't been set yet.

10. Withdrawing credits and staying a member drafted, exchange rate source still open

There is no fixed programme cycle. Membership is ongoing - a partner joins once and stays a member indefinitely, with no end date forcing a settlement. This is a deliberate change from Sarthy's original cycle-based design.

  • Minimum reserve: a partner must always keep at least their original entry amount (999 credits) in their wallet.
  • Withdrawing: anything above the reserve can be withdrawn quarterly. Credits convert to real currency at whatever the live exchange rate is on the day of withdrawal, not the rate from when the partner joined.
  • The 9% fee: charged once, at the moment credit transfers to a referring partner on conversion (Section 6) - not a second time at withdrawal, since it's already been taken at the source.
  • Pausing or stepping away: no cycle to leave mid-way through means no forfeiture, no prorated payout. A quiet account just sits as-is until the partner returns.

No partner should be told their credit balance is guaranteed cash before an actual withdrawal is processed.

Still open: which specific exchange rate source counts as the official reference at the moment of payout.

11. How we group partners - Giver, Taker, or Balanced drafted

Three patterns, not two. Some partners actively contribute to the ecosystem (Givers), some mostly draw from it without reciprocating (Takers), and most sit somewhere in between (Balanced) - giving and receiving in roughly even measure. This shows up directly in the credit wallet: a steadily growing balance behaves like a Giver, a steadily shrinking one behaves like a Taker, and a balance that moves in both directions without a clear trend is Balanced. Sarthy uses this trend when reviewing partners periodically, and may deprioritize or exit partners who consistently show Taker behavior. Most partners are expected to sit in Balanced most of the time - it's the default, not an edge case.

The same judgment applies earlier too, during onboarding - client base health, professional standing, and whether the partner seems collaborative rather than purely extractive. Not a hard checklist - a conversation-based read during the discovery call.

See the full Partner Segmentation Framework for the priority tiers this feeds into.

13. Guardrails against abuse confirmed

These are not allowed, under any circumstance:

  • Submitting fake or made-up leads to earn credits
  • Submitting leads for contacts with no real interest in the receiving partner's services
  • Two partners coordinating to exchange fake leads with each other
  • Claiming a conversion when no paid work has actually started
  • Accepting a lead with no intention of following up, purely to deny the referrer's credit
  • Submitting a lead already engaged elsewhere in the network

Sarthy periodically reviews referral patterns network-wide - consistently low conversion, or suspicious coordination between just one or two partners, gets flagged for review. Not automatically punitive, a health check. Serious fraud can be escalated to legal action.

19. How we measure success drafted, proposal awaiting confirmation

Sarthy's own scorecard: finding and signing up partners (20%), supporting startups (40%), matching partners to startups (30%), research and reporting (10%). The last two weren't clearly defined in Sarthy's own records - proposed measures below, grounded in what this SOP actually tracks.

Match-Making (30%):

  • Leads matched to at least one relevant partner each month
  • Average time from submission to reaching a partner, against the 5-working-day target
  • Share of leads validated and matched versus rejected or stalled
  • Whether leads reach a healthy spread of partners, not just the same one or two

Research & Reports (10%):

  • A recurring report - monthly to start - on partner and lead activity trends
  • Written recommendations from that report, and whether they get acted on
  • Partner feedback gathered regularly and rolled into the same reporting

20. After the deal closes - the referral fee drafted

Everything in Sections 5-10 covers the 999-point credit exchanged when a lead is shared. This is a separate, second reward that only kicks in once a shared lead becomes a real signed deal - running in parallel, not replacing the credit system.

  1. The receiving partner closes the lead, signing an agreement directly with the startup.
  2. The deal's value and duration is shared with Sarthy and the referring partner.
  3. The referring partner is entitled to 10% of the value actually invoiced during the first 12 months of the engagement.
  4. This flows through Sarthy - Sarthy takes a 9% cut of that 10%, the same 9% principle used everywhere in this document. The referring partner nets 91% of the 10%, which works out to 9.1% of what was invoiced in that first year.
  5. The amount accumulates over the year and pays out once annually, at financial year-end (March 31st).
  6. The entitlement lasts 1 year from the lead's first engagement with the receiving partner. After that year, the two continue their relationship directly - no further obligation back to the referrer, and no further tri-party arrangement with Sarthy on that relationship.

Why deal_duration matters: if an engagement runs longer than 12 months (say, an 18-month retainer), only what's actually invoiced in the first 12 months counts toward the referrer's entitlement - not the full multi-year contract value. deal_duration is what tells Sarthy whether a deal needs this year-one proration at all, or whether the full deal_value already falls within the 1-year window.

If the same startup later engages a different partner - a genuinely separate referral to someone else, not a continuation - that's a fresh, independent deal with its own 12-month entitlement clock, unrelated to any prior engagement with a different partner.

21. Staying an active partner drafted

A partner who doesn't share any lead for 3 consecutive months is marked dormant - removed from the public listing and blocked from receiving leads until they resume activity. The underlying principle: no partner gets to be purely a taker, permanently receiving without ever contributing - connects directly to the Giver/Taker framing in Section 11. Gamifying this further (leaderboards, streaks, recognition) is a real direction as the platform matures, not designed yet.

22. Frequently asked questions confirmed

Do I lose my starting 999 credits if none of my referrals convert? No - opening credits aren't tied to referral performance. Receiving leads still deducts 999 credits regardless.

What if I refer a lead and it doesn't convert? No credit earned. The receiving partner's deduction stands either way.

Can I decline a lead I don't think is relevant? Yes - before the deduction happens. Outside your service area, decline and nothing gets deducted.

Can I refer more than one lead to the same partner? Yes - each referral is its own separate transaction.

What if a lead converts months later? The credit is awarded whenever conversion is actually confirmed.

23. Closing note confirmed

This program is built on the belief that the strongest partner networks run on mutual accountability, not just shared access. Every partner who joins becomes a real stakeholder in a shared system - not a passive vendor waiting for the phone to ring. The credit model is deliberately simple and the rules are deliberately transparent, so that a partner's balance is an honest reflection of what they've actually put into the ecosystem. This program works when every partner is both a generous contributor and a serious professional.
