This is the honest picture of how things work today, based on real documents and the tracking sheet - not guesses. Organized around Sarthy's own way of measuring this work, so it's easy for them to relate to.

1. The path to today

The partner program has gone through three real stages. Today's reality is a mix of all three at once: old agreements still technically active, a redesign that was written but never fully built, and a very early version of the actual app.

2. How many partners are actually engaged - the real starting point

By Sarthy's own account, the original agreement "lacked a call to action," so out of everyone who signed up, fewer than 15% ever became active. That's the number any new plan should be measured against - not zero, since something was already tried and mostly didn't stick.

There's also a real, waiting group of roughly 25 more partners - in India and abroad - already asking for access to Sarthy's startups but not yet signed up. That's a separate opportunity from re-engaging the old, inactive group.

3. Finding and signing up partners - today's reality

New partners come through LinkedIn, mass emails, a WhatsApp group, personal connections, and direct referrals - there's no single defined channel. Deciding whether someone's a good fit is an informal judgment call made during a conversation, not a written checklist. Once someone signs, the process today is: sign the agreement, upload a scanned copy to a shared drive, assign them a code, and add their offer to a shared spreadsheet. There's no step yet that actually gets a new partner set up and using the app.

4. Supporting startups - today's reality

Startups are tracked in a separate part of the same spreadsheet, using the same manual approach: mass emails, phone calls, and a short scoring exercise called the "5-minute test." Everything is logged by hand, by whichever team member happens to be active. There's no system connecting a startup's actual need to which partners get suggested to them - it depends on someone remembering.

5. Matching partners to startups - today's reality

This is the least documented part of Sarthy's own scorecard. There's no written definition yet of what "good matching" actually looks like, or how to measure it. In practice, it happens because a team member happens to remember which partner suits which startup - it isn't tracked anywhere as a specific, countable action. Confirmed as a real gap by the Lead, who will write a specific set of rules for what "good matching" means and how to measure it in an upcoming update - deliberately left open here until then, rather than guessed at now.

6. Research and reporting - today's reality

Also thin. There's no regular research schedule or report format in use today. This part of Sarthy's scorecard seems to exist on paper more than in practice right now.

7. The biggest single gap - getting partners onto the app

The partner tracking sheet has a column literally called "Part of Platform" - and for almost every partner, it's blank. This is the clearest evidence available: the app exists, but almost nobody who signed up in the first phase has ever actually been moved onto it.

Confirmed directly with the Lead: even among the small number who are on the platform, real usage is infrequent, not active. So this isn't just an onboarding gap - it's also an activation gap, once someone is already in. Whatever new onboarding process gets built, closing both is the main job.

8. Money arrangements - more variety than expected

Most agreements follow the standard 10% mutual referral fee, with a 12-month tail after either side leaves, and no exclusivity. But real signed exceptions exist. One partner has a reverse 60/40 profit-sharing deal for sales Sarthy brings in directly. Another gets a flat ₹10,000 bonus per converted trial, capped at two years. A third has a tiered commission instead of a flat fee - 15% or 20%, depending on deal size. Any new onboarding process needs to work for these exceptions too, not just the standard case.

9. What's already decided, and what's still open

This section used to list four things as still open for Sarthy. All four are now resolved and written up elsewhere in the hub: how credits and currency convert and exactly how the 9% fee applies (Partnership SOP v1.0 Section 10), how disputes get resolved (Section 9), and what happens if the Alpha rollout target is missed (Alpha to Beta Graduation Criteria - resolved as manually handled case by case, not a hardcoded rule). Nothing is genuinely open here any more; this section is kept only for the historical record of what used to be unconfirmed.

The one rule that was already clear at the time still holds: a partner who breaks trust gets blocked, and if a lead gets rejected, the credit is automatically reversed. The full dispute-handling process now lives in Partnership SOP v1.0 Section 9.

10. What this means for the work ahead

See Partnership SOP v1.0 for how these findings translate into actual rules and sections.

  • Making onboarding and engagement work better: the bar to beat is under 15% engagement from the first phase - this is a fix, not a cold start.
  • Getting everything to run through the app: the empty "Part of Platform" column is the single clearest, most measurable target for this whole project.
  • Getting partners into the habit of using the app: there's no existing data on this yet, since almost no one uses it. What "a good habit" looks like here still needs defining, based directly on what the early-user interviews reveal.
