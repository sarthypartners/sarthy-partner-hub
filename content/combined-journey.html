A. The Referral Timeline

This section lines up the startup, the referring partner, and the receiving partner against the same shared timeline for one specific referral, so it's possible to see what each of them is experiencing at the exact same moment. Built to spot where engagement quietly breaks down, as the first step before designing any fix.

Before this timeline starts, both partners have already been through their own account lifecycle - see Sections B and C below. This section picks up once a specific startup enters the picture, and follows that one lead through to conversion, deal closure, and whatever happens next.

What this reveals

Looking at any one lane alone, the design holds together. Lining up all three at once surfaces three findings worth confirming with Sarthy before designing engagement fixes:

Finding 1 (narrower since the connection-flow rewrite) - a consent decision, once made, still isn't communicated to the referring partner.

This finding has changed since Partnership SOP v1.0 Section 7 and the Lead state machine were rewritten around the confirmed Connection Request Flow. The failure mode this finding originally centered on - a lead quietly timing out with no message to anyone - no longer exists: there's no more auto-expiry of any kind, and every request now becomes visible to all relevant parties immediately, as a faded, FYI-only "Introduced" entry (see that document for why the visibility is deliberately inactive rather than full notice). What's left, checked directly against the Communication Template Library's message triggers, is smaller but still real: once admin's consent conversation concludes - whether the receiving partner (or, in directions A and C, the startup) gives or refuses consent - only template 3 exists, and it's addressed to the receiving partner only. Nobody currently tells the referring partner which way it went, or that it resolved at all, beyond the faded FYI entry they could already see for themselves. (The existing internal Associate nudge, template 4, remains wired to a separate, later stall point - a lead that's been Acknowledged and then goes quiet during the actual work - and is unaffected by any of this.)

Finding 2 - there's no message for the moment a referral actually pays off.

The Communication Template Library has a reminder before the acknowledgment window closes (template 2) and an annual referral-fee payout notice many months later (template 10). Nothing sits between them. The moment a referring partner's lead actually converts and 909 credits land in their wallet - arguably the single most motivating event in the whole credit system - currently has no defined message of its own.

Finding 3 - the startup's own repeat engagement isn't tracked anywhere.

The Data Dictionary tracks a partner's activity in detail - giving_pattern, last_lead_shared_date, dormancy status. Nothing equivalent exists for a startup. If a startup that came through once needs a different kind of service later, whether it comes back through the same referring partner, self-serves again through Path B, or just leaves the ecosystem entirely isn't something any document currently answers or measures. Section D below is a first, deliberately unconfirmed attempt at naming what that would even look like.

These are observations from lining up existing, already-confirmed rules against each other - not new rules, and not confirmed defects. Worth raising with Sarthy directly rather than assumed either way.

Built from

This map doesn't introduce any new rule. Every stage traces back to something already confirmed elsewhere: the Lead state machine in the Low-Level System Specification, the 3-direction connection flow and admin-mediated consent process in Partnership SOP v1.0, Section 7 (described in more detail in the confirmed Connection Request Flow), the entity relationships in the Data Dictionary, and the message inventory in the Communication Template Library. The three findings above come only from placing those existing facts side by side - Finding 1 has been narrowed to match the confirmed rewrite; Findings 2 and 3 are unaffected by it.

What comes next

This section is diagnostic, not prescriptive - it maps where engagement currently breaks down, without yet proposing what should replace the gaps. Designing the actual fixes (new message templates, a startup-facing retention signal, or something else) is deliberately a separate, later step, once these three findings are confirmed rather than assumed.

B. Referring Partner Lifecycle

Sections B and C describe the same underlying partner account lifecycle - the same eight stages, the same mechanics (account status, wallet trend, dormancy rules). They're split into two sections because a partner's day-to-day experience genuinely differs in a few concrete places - most clearly, whether "first activity" means submitting a lead or receiving one. Everywhere else, the two sections say the same thing in the same words on purpose, not by oversight. This is the full detail behind Partnership SOP v1.0 #14.

What the interview guide should validate

Sections B and C are built from documented rules, not direct partner testimony. The Alpha Partner Interview Guide exists specifically to check whether Stage 4 and 5 actually feel this way to a real partner, or whether the friction sits somewhere these sections haven't captured yet.

D. Startup Lifecycle Proposed - not yet validated

This section is new content, not migrated from any existing hub document. Nothing in the hub today defines a startup's own lifecycle across time, separate from any single referral - Section A only follows one startup through one referral. The four stages below are reasoned by analogy to Section A and to the Referring/Receiving Partner Lifecycles, directly answering Finding 3 ("the startup's own repeat engagement isn't tracked anywhere") - they are not sourced from a confirmed Sarthy rule, and shouldn't be treated as fact until checked against real startup behaviour or confirmed with Sarthy.

2. EngagedActively working with a receiving partner on this specific needMatches Section A, Stages 3-4 - well defined
3. ConvertedThe deal closes; the startup becomes the receiving partner's own clientMatches Section A, Stage 5 - well defined
