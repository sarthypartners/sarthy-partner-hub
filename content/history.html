This page holds the "why" and the "what it used to be" - the history behind decisions in the hub. The other 24 pages describe what's true today. This page is where that older context lives instead, so it doesn't get in the way of a reader just trying to find out the current rule.

Why Partnership SOP v1.0 absorbed Sarthy's Playbook 2.0

Sarthy had already written a detailed design document called Playbook 2.0. It covered years of their own thinking on the credit system, the referral flow, and the rules against abuse.

Early in this project, that document sat outside the hub as a separate reference. Partnership SOP v1.0 only pointed to it.

Later, this was judged to create a real risk: two documents describing the same rules, with no guarantee they'd stay in sync. So every real piece of Playbook 2.0 was rewritten into Partnership SOP v1.0, section by section. Playbook 2.0 is now retired. Nothing about the program lives there that isn't also in the SOP.

Why Sarthy's original Playbook 1.0 was never absorbed

Playbook 1.0 was Sarthy's very first version of the program, from 2023-24. It was much simpler than Playbook 2.0 - mostly just a flat 10% referral fee, no credit wallet. Almost everything worth knowing from it is already captured in the Current-State Assessment's history section, so it was never pulled into the SOP the way Playbook 2.0 was.

Why the Master Execution Sequence's item 11 covers four different things

Item 11 used to be four separate items on the list: a new onboarding process, an engagement plan, a reactivation plan, and an escalation SOP. They were merged into one item. All four were always meant to become sections inside Partnership SOP v1.0, not standalone documents - keeping them as four separate to-do items was creating duplicate work.

Why two documents in the hub aren't numbered on the Sequence page

The Glossary and the Action Item & Blocker Tracker don't have numbers on the Master Execution Sequence page. That's not a gap - they simply weren't part of the original plan. Both came out of later requests as the hub grew.

Why the Gantt view and the Migration Wave Plan were retired

Two documents were cut outright, not just rewritten. The Project Plan (Gantt View) duplicated the Roadmap's own phase timeline - the two disagreed on Phase 2/3/4 durations by about a week each, and reconciling them added a second source of truth for the same information rather than removing one. Its other real content, a "what's actually still ahead" list, already substantially duplicated the Action Item & Blocker Tracker. Cut in favor of keeping the Roadmap as the single timeline view - it now carries a status column (Done / In Progress / Not started) so nothing about real-world progress is lost.

The Migration Wave Plan was retired because real partner migration is now treated as a separate activity, taken up only once the platform itself is built - not a document worth actively maintaining alongside build-stage work. Its specific gate for "Wave 2" (special-agreement partners) never actually matched the Graduation Criteria's own readiness gate, despite the dependency map once claiming they were linked - reconciling that mismatch stopped being worth the effort once migration itself moved out of scope. What was worth keeping from it lives on: wave-sequencing-by-engagement-history is still true of the Segmentation Framework's outreach-priority logic, and the unresolved 9%-fee-vs-60/40-split question for special-agreement partners is still tracked in the Action Item & Blocker Tracker's legal-review item. Whoever picks migration planning back up later should start from the Graduation Criteria and the Action Tracker, not recreate a new version of this document from scratch without checking those first.

Why the partner network changed from a one-way referral model

The first version of the partner program (2023-24) only flowed one way. Sarthy referred startups to partners. Partners never referred business to each other.

The redesign turned this into a network where any partner can refer to, and receive from, any other partner - with Sarthy tracking it all.

What changedFirst versionCurrent design
StructureSarthy to partner onlyAny partner to any partner, with Sarthy tracking everything
Cost to joinFree₹999 (India) or $49 (international)
Credit walletNoneStarts with 999 credits, earned and spent through referrals
How partners earnOnly from their own client workCredits for every referral that converts, plus their own client work
Cost of receiving a leadNone999 credits deducted per lead received
AccountabilitySigned agreement onlySigned agreement plus a tracked credit wallet and conversion record

In one line: in the old model, a partner was a vendor. In the current design, a partner is a node in a shared network, where everyone has something to gain and something to manage.

Why membership has no fixed cycle

Sarthy's original design was built around cycles - a membership period that ended in a settlement event. This SOP made a deliberate change: membership is now ongoing, with no cycle and no forced end date. A partner joins once and stays a member indefinitely.

Why the Segmentation Framework changed from 5 tiers to 2 fields

The Segmentation Framework used to be a single 5-tier list. It reused words like "At-Risk" and "Dormant" - the same words already meaning something different in the Data Dictionary's account status field. A reader seeing "At-Risk" in a spreadsheet couldn't tell whether it meant the segmentation tier or the account status.

It was redesigned into two separate fields with no overlapping words: status (what the account can do) and giving_pattern (how the partner behaves, only meaningful when status is Active).
